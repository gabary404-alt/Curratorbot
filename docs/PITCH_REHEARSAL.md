# Currator pitch rehearsal

**Opening line**  
Currator is a conversational robot built for Rwanda. It answers in the visitor’s
language, looks up sources when needed, and speaks its reply.

## Demo order

1. Open the console in Auto detect and Auto sources. Ask aloud “Muraho! Witwa nde?”
   Check the transcript, Kinyarwanda reply, and audio through the actual speakers.
2. Ask “If we share 12 mangoes among four people, how many does each get, and why?”
   Expect three each and a short explanation. Use the same microphone and settings.
3. Ask “What are the latest news headlines about Rwanda?” Open one source and
   check that the answer reflects the linked reporting. If sources fail, show that
   Currator can admit it cannot verify the answer.
4. Show one supervised movement only after firmware, obstacle handling, and physical
   emergency stop pass the hardware contract. Keep the conversational demo available
   as a stationary presentation.

## Rehearse with the actual setup

- Ask three Kinyarwanda speakers to try introductions, follow-ups, and a switch to
  English. Record misheard words and repeat outdoors or in a room with comparable noise.
- Time the first reply, then repeat the sequence three times on the event network.
  Check browser autoplay, volume, wake from sleep, and quota after repeated turns.
- Rehearse typed questions, the Kinyarwanda selector with Pindo transcription, and
  clearly labeled rehearsal responses as fallbacks.
- Keep the demo password with the operator. Confirm the presentation browser is
  signed in and has microphone permission before going on stage.

## Inputs still needed

- Pitch date, venue, allotted speaking time, and whether the pitch is for a class,
  judges, or customers.
- Robot computer and OS, ESP32 firmware owner and USB protocol confirmation,
  actual microphone and speaker, and when the complete unit is available for a bench test.
- A native speaker’s approval of Kinyarwanda captions and pronunciation, and team
  approval of the English voice on the presentation speakers.

Claim the features that pass rehearsal on the actual setup. Describe other languages
as text support until they have their own voice rehearsal. Present news results as
headlines with sources. Explain that movement is supervised if you demonstrate it.
